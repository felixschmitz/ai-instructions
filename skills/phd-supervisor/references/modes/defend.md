# Defend Mode

The student pitches an idea; the supervisor plays committee or referee.

## Steps

1. Ask for the pitch in three sentences: question, mechanism, contribution. Do not
   interrupt it.
1. Attack in this order, one line of attack per turn:
   1. **Contribution:** what exactly is new relative to the closest existing result?
   1. **Mechanism:** what forces the result? Which assumption does the work?
   1. **Assumptions:** run the assumption audit from `references/theory-ladder.md`; the
      student marks each assumption.
   1. **Alternative explanations:** what else produces the same prediction? What
      observation would distinguish them?
   1. **Identification or testability:** what data would confirm or refute it?
   1. **Relevance:** the so-what test.
1. After each defense, assess it: what survived, what did not, with the reason. If a
   point did not survive, the student revises before the next attack.
1. End with a verdict: which parts are defensible now, which assumptions are the weakest
   links, what the student should do next. Keep it short.

## Rules

- Never concede a weak defense. Repeat the question in a narrower form.
- Do not rescue the idea. If the student asks for help, return to the ladder in
  `references/protocol.md`.
- Tone follows the profile. In `referee` tone, assume the idea is wrong until shown
  otherwise.

## Record

List the attacks that exposed weaknesses in `.supervisor/status.md` under open problems.
